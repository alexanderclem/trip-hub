The north star
The feeling: the most prepared person in the group has everything covered, and everyone else immediately knows what to do. Warm and quietly confident, like a superb consumer travel app. Mobile first, emotionally grounded in travel but exceptionally functional under stress. Beautiful enough to stand beside mature consumer products, never a sterile B2B dashboard, generic template, or flashy AI-generated website.

The product mental model: A trip is one shared living plan. In planning, people propose, save, vote, and agree. In preparing, they confirm details, gather tickets, coordinate arrivals, and prepare offline. In traveling, they need the next stop, meeting place, timing, transport guidance, ticket, offline availability, and expense entry instantly. In completed, they settle costs and revisit their trip. The application may react to phase and personal needs, but navigation must remain predictable and destinations stable.

Primary users: (1) the friend organizing a trip for ~4–12 people, (2) a participant joining through a group-chat link, (3) a traveler opening the app on an iPhone in a stressful/offline moment. Secondary: organizer/retreat/operator workflows; preserve these, but do not let them dominate the free consumer app or homepage.

Real product promises: group decisions; shared places; genuinely editable day-by-day itinerary; accessible tickets/confirmations; split expenses and settle-up suggestions; prepared offline use; no required account or installation for invitees. Important boundaries: voting expresses preferences, it does NOT automatically book or autonomously commit a winner to the itinerary; Stowaway does not make bookings, does not move money between friends, and offline maps/tickets require prior preparation on that device. Do not introduce misleading claims.

Design rules — what 'professional grade' must mean here
- Clarity before decoration. The user sees the most relevant next action in the first useful viewport. A beautiful screen should also be dramatically easier to scan.
- One dominant job and primary action per surface. Prioritize based on context, not number of features. Avoid two floating + buttons, duplicate polls, duplicated summary cards, dense dashboards of equal-weight rectangles.
- Treat typography, spacing, and alignment as a system. Distinct display / page title / section / record / metadata / status roles; consistently aligned content; tabular times and money; restrained line lengths; proper wrapping. Base density on the job: compact itinerary, spacious first-run, prominent next action.
- Brand-specific restraint. Keep existing ink, print, perforation and route-code motifs ONLY when they help orientation or make a rare memorable moment. No glossy glassmorphism, gradient blobs, neon, purple SaaS dashboards, ornamental icon grids, meaningless badges, forced serif headings, over-rounded pills, pointless charts, or endless explanatory copy.
- Progressive disclosure with integrity. Hide advanced fields, optional details and secondary actions behind a clearly named expandable affordance; do not hide crucial confirmation, payment, conflict, privacy or offline warnings.
- Actions communicate outcomes. Distinguish proposed / voted / selected / planned / tentative / booked / saved locally / uploading / synced / failed / downloaded on this phone. These are NOT interchangeable.
- Motion has a purpose. Immediate feedback; occasional spatial continuity for a moved plan item or changed vote; brief interruptible transitions; no entrance choreography on every return, no animated balances, no autoplay UI film that pretends to be interactive.
- Mobile is the primary artifact. Optimize 390x844 and 320px; then build a genuinely useful desktop shell rather than stretching a phone stack into a wide canvas.
- Hard real-world cases matter. Long trip names, 15+ day trips, many participants, overlapping activities, incomplete destinations, multiple currencies, incomplete tickets, pending sync, device storage denial, no map pack, slow and interrupted networks, no account, access denied, no results, and legacy saved data.
- Respect what already works. Current shared control semantics, keyboard-oriented Plan day tabs, local-first sync, native modal accessibility, and existing mobile simplification are strengths to retain or improve.

Quality gate — the outcomes to optimize
- A first-time organizer can create a trip and understand how to invite friends without navigating a complicated setup wizard.
- A participant opening a link can join and identify the group's plan without being forced to install, sign up, answer a quiz, or comprehend the application's entire feature taxonomy.
- A returning user can identify their highest-priority trip action in about three seconds in a representative usability test; this is a research goal, not a claimed measured result.
- A traveler can access an upcoming ticket, see a real departure estimate, or find an offline-ready indication without hunting through five destinations.
- Actions are discoverable and predictable; deep-linked screens work without first visiting Home.
- No visual polish introduces data-loss risk, incorrect financial numbers, permissions regression, or false offline/sync assurances.
- Screens feel considered at both sparse and dense data states and at phone and desktop sizes.
Do not turn this into an endless audit. The sequence includes one bounded discovery phase and then real implementation.
